
Since you’ve already established the **"Thinker/Producer"** split, and you’re focusing on **atomic notes** in Obsidian while on your Spring 2026 leave, the goal is to shift the tablet from a "consumption device" to a "cognitive sandbox."

"Thinking" in research—especially regarding complex K-12 disciplinary disparities—often requires **spatial reasoning** (seeing how policies connect to outcomes) and **low-friction capture** (getting thoughts out before the academic "editor" in your brain kills them).

Here are four tools/plugins you likely don’t have in your stack that are built specifically to encourage "thinking" on a tablet:

### 1. The Visual Linker: ExcaliBrain (Obsidian Plugin)

If Obsidian is your second brain, **ExcaliBrain** is the visual cortex. While the native Canvas is good for manual layouts, ExcaliBrain automatically maps the relationships between your atomic notes based on your links.

- **Why it encourages thinking:** As a jazz pianist, you likely think in patterns and intervals. ExcaliBrain lets you "see" the intervals between your research concepts. It allows you to navigate your 13,000+ notes visually with the S-Pen, helping you spot "missing links" in your dissertation's logic that text-only searching would miss.
    
- **Local-First Status:** 100% local (it’s just a way of viewing your existing `.md` files).
    

### 2. The Infinite Sandbox: Concepts (App)

Obsidian is structured; **Concepts** is unstructured. It is an infinite-canvas sketching app that is "vector-based," meaning you can move and resize your handwriting after you've written it.

- **Why it encourages thinking:** Sometimes a "note" is too formal. When you're trying to decode the "why" behind a specific state mandate, you need to scribble, draw arrows, and move blocks around. You can then select your "finished" conceptual drawing and drag-and-drop it directly into an Obsidian note.
    
- **Local-First Status:** Files are stored locally on your Tab S10 Ultra.
    

### 3. The "Voice-to-Atomic" Flow: WhisperWriter (Local Android AI)

Since you run Qwen and Ollama, you appreciate the power of local inference. **WhisperWriter** (or similar on-device Whisper ports) allows you to dictate your thoughts directly on the tablet using OpenAI’s Whisper model **locally**.

- **Why it encourages thinking:** Academic writing is often stifled by the keyboard. Use the tablet's microphone to "talk through" a difficult paragraph while walking. Because it’s local, it’s instant and private. You can then save that transcript directly into your Obsidian `Capture` folder.
    
- **Local-First Status:** No cloud involved; it uses the NPU (Neural Processing Unit) on your Tab S10 Ultra.
    

### 4. The Deep Reader: LiquidText (Local Mode)

For a PhD candidate, "thinking" often happens _on_ the PDF. **LiquidText** is unique because it allows you to "pinch" a document to bring two distant pages together or "pull" excerpts out into a workspace where they remain linked to the source.

- **Why it encourages thinking:** It mimics the way a researcher spreads papers out on a physical desk. You can pull a quote from a policy document and an excerpt from a data report into a central workspace, then draw a line between them with your S-Pen to "bridge" the thought.
    
- **Local-First Status:** It has a robust "Local Only" mode that keeps your research data off their servers.
